Skip bin permits in Mackay: Mackay Regional Council rules

In short: What Mackay Regional Council requires, how to apply, and where to place a skip on your own property.

A skip on your own driveway usually doesn't need council approval. A skip on the road, footpath or nature strip is different. Here's what Mackay Regional Council says and what to check before you book.

Last updated: 24 October 2026

The short answer

On your own land, generally no. On a road or footway, yes: Mackay Regional Council requires an "Approval for works on public areas and roads". Its 2025–26 fees list $369 for the category that covers rubbish skips.

On your own property

Your own driveway, yard or a firm patch of ground is the easiest place for a skip. Council's approval is for skips on a road or footway, so a bin wholly on your property generally doesn't need it. After heavy rain, lawns get soft, so a driveway or hardstand is safer for the truck.

On the road, footpath or nature strip

Mackay Regional Council requires an "Approval for works on public areas and roads" to place a skip on a road or footway, under its Subordinate Local Laws 1.1 and 1.16. Council's 2025–26 fees list $369 for the "Alteration/improvements: shipping containers, rubbish skips etc." category. Fees change each financial year, so confirm the current amount.

Before you book a skip on the street, check with Mackay Regional Council on 1300 622 529 or council@mackay.qld.gov.au, or visit the council website. Mention it on the quote form too, because the operator may be able to advise.

What to ask council before you book

  1. Does my spot need an "Approval for works on public areas and roads", and should I or the skip operator apply?
  2. What does it cost this financial year, and how long does assessment take?
  3. How long can the skip stay, and are there conditions such as lights, reflectors or a set distance from the kerb?
  4. Are there extra rules for a busy road, a bus stop or a heritage area?

Ask for the answers in writing or note the date and who you spoke to.

Placing the skip safely

Keep the skip clear of driveways, hydrants, bus stops and drains, and don't fill above the rim. Given council's fee for skips on public land, it's usually worth finding room on your own property.
